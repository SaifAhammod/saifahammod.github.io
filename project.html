  1. NIRO Educational Robot

    Robotics
    PYTHON, C++, ARDUINO, RASPBERRY PI, LINUX, FUSION360, PCB, FRITZING
    The purpose of an educational robot is to facilitate research and provide students with practical experience in robotics within academic settings. Since different research areas demand varying hardware configurations, a flexible and customizable platform is essential. To address this need, we present the NIRO EDU Bot, a fully designed and fabricated robot developed at the NSU Intelligent Robotics Lab. It comes with built-in components such as a gyroscope, IR sensors, encoder motors, WiFi connectivity, and integrates both Raspberry Pi and Arduino for processing. Additionally, users can enhance the system with configurable hardware like LiDAR, depth cameras, microphone arrays, and AprilTags based on their research requirements. The robot features a 3D-designed body, a custom-built motherboard for a compact structure, and is built on a differential drive mechanism.


  2. ADOAVER: An Autonomous Dynamic Obstacle Avoiding Exploring Robot

    Robotics
    PYTHON, C++, ARDUINO, RASPBERRY PI, LINUX, FUSION360, PCB, FRITZING, ROS
    This study presents the development of ADOAVER (Autonomous Dynamic Obstacle Avoiding Exploring Robot), an advanced robotic system designed for autonomous navigation and exploration in complex and inaccessible environments. ADOAVER is capable of detecting and avoiding both static and dynamic obstacles, ensuring efficient movement in challenging terrains. The system integrates multiple hardware and software components to achieve three fundamental objectives: automation, dynamic obstacle avoidance, and environmental exploration.
    System Components and Implementation:
    1. Hardware Architecture: ADOAVER is built using a custom-designed robotic platform equipped with LIDAR sensors for real-time mapping and obstacle detection, encoder motors for precise movement control, and a Raspberry Pi as the main processing unit. These components work together to enable autonomous decision-making and adaptive navigation.
    2. Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance: The robot utilizes Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) to generate real-time maps of its surroundings. By continuously analyzing LIDAR data and encoder feedback, ADOAVER detects and responds to both moving and stationary obstacles, allowing for efficient path planning and adaptive movement.
    3. Exploration and Automation: The system is designed to autonomously explore unknown areas, making it particularly useful in hazardous or hard-to-reach locations. By leveraging SLAM and sensor fusion techniques, ADOAVER constructs an accurate environmental model, enabling it to navigate effectively without human intervention.
    Conclusion: By integrating advanced sensing, localization, and obstacle avoidance techniques, ADOAVER enhances robotic mobility and exploration capabilities. Its real-time adaptability makes it a valuable tool for search and rescue missions, environmental monitoring, and industrial automation.

  3. Shouhardo

    Human Computing Interaction (HCI)
    Human-Centered Solution Design, Problem Analysis, Communication Skills, Wordpress
    During the later phase of the COVID-19 pandemic, we conducted a qualitative study by interviewing approximately 32 randomly selected individuals to understand their experiences during hospital visits. The findings revealed three major challenges faced by patients. First, most visitors lacked clear information about the hospital layout, such as the location of departments and the services available in each, which often led them to fall victim to middlemen exploiting their situation. Second, patients were frequently unaware of the actual prices of medicines, which resulted in them being overcharged and deceived by unethical vendors. Third, many patients struggled to identify the appropriate doctor for their specific health condition, leading to delays in receiving proper treatment.
    From these insights, we designed a Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)-driven web-based solution focused on Rajshahi Medical College Hospital to address these issues. The platform, accessible in multiple languages, aims to provide a seamless and user-friendly interface that allows patients to find accurate, reliable, and up-to-date medical facility information without the interference of third parties. Key features include step-by-step guidance on outdoor hospital treatment procedures, doctor appointment booking, real-time availability of specialized doctors, location and cost details of medical supply stores, and other essential hospital-related services. By integrating usability principles and inclusive design, this system enhances accessibility, reduces patient confusion, and empowers individuals to make informed healthcare decisions.

  6. ROBO SOCCER

    Robotics
    ARDUINO, C, SKETCHUP
    Our RoboSoccer project evolved through three distinct design iterations, each addressing the limitations of its predecessor. The first prototype was built using a 5 mm PVC board chassis, powered by six 200 RPM Pololu motors connected to a single motor driver and controlled via an Arduino Uno with a FlySky 6-channel transmitter. While functional, this version suffered from low power output and severe motor driver overheating.

    To improve performance, the second version employed a combination of 3 mm plywood and 5 mm PVC board for weight optimization, featuring two high-torque motors and two high-RPM motors. This time, we integrated dual motor drivers equipped with cooling fans. Although the system delivered higher torque, it was noticeably slower, despite the increased power capacity.

    Our final and most successful version was constructed entirely from 3 mm plywood, housing six 400 RPM motors with custom-modified wheels for enhanced mobility. Both cooling fans were upgraded with protective covers, resulting in a smooth and highly responsive drive system. This version, nicknamed “Night Fury”, delivered exceptional handling and speed, becoming the standout performer of our RoboSoccer development journey.
